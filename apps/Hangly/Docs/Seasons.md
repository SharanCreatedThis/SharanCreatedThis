# Seasonal packs

Eleven charms in four sets, and a rope that puts them on when the season comes round.

| Pack | Charms | When |
|---|---|---|
| Halloween | Bat, Ghost, Pumpkin | 1–31 October |
| Diwali | Lotus, Lantern, Diya | Editable; early November by default |
| Christmas | Snowflake, Candy Cane, Bell | 1–26 December |
| New Year | Firework, Lucky Coin | 27 December – 6 January |

Christmas stops on Boxing Day and New Year takes the next morning, so no day is
claimed twice — a day claimed twice would be a day where which charm you got
depended on the order of an enum. Diwali is not on a fixed date because it moves
with the lunar calendar, so it lives in settings where it can be corrected, and it
outranks a fixed window where the two overlap: it is the one the user set by hand.

## How the artwork was made

**These were constructed, not illustrated, and that is a real difference.** The
eleven collection charms are Adobe Illustrator drawings — twenty-five to a hundred
and eight paths of dense bezier work each. The seasonal charms are generated by
[`Scripts/generate-seasonal-charms.py`](../Scripts/generate-seasonal-charms.py) from
one shared style kit, because a snowflake's six-fold symmetry and a coin's
concentric rims are arithmetic, and arithmetic is better written down than clicked.

They hold their own against the collection at a glance and do not match it under
inspection. Every one is a drop-in replacement: put a drawn SVG in `Assets/Charms`
under the same name, run `Scripts/sync-charm-assets.sh`, and nothing else changes.

The style kit is what makes them sit together and sit beside the collection:

- Light from the upper left, in every charm, without exception.
- A dark contour under every shape, a gradient fill over it, a specular on top.
- The collection's gold on the cord and the beads.
- Gradients rather than many flat paths. The collection builds its shading out of
  dozens of solid fills; macOS renders SVG gradients properly, so these get the same
  richness from a tenth of the geometry.

### What the artwork has to obey

Three conventions, all of them enforced by `CharmArtworkSplitterTests` and all three
of which the first drafts broke:

1. **The cord runs the whole way down, and the beads sit on it.** The splitter reads
   the artwork as rows and calls a narrow row cord and a wide one a bead — so the
   narrow rows *between* the beads are what say there are two beads rather than one
   long blob. Beads with nothing between them merge and the split fails outright.
2. **Nothing the body reaches up with may touch the beads.** A bell's crown and a
   pumpkin's stalk both did, and both merged the bead into the body.
3. **The charm fills its canvas and is taller than it is wide.** They hang, so they
   are portrait; the lotus and the bat were drawn landscape and had to be brought
   upright.

## Dressing and undressing

The undressing is what makes the dressing acceptable. Changing what somebody chose
is a liberty; changing it back on the first of November is what turns it into a
decoration rather than a nuisance. So:

- What was on the rope is written into **settings** before a pack takes over, and
  survives a reboot. Automatic dressing must not be a feature that quietly eats the
  charm you chose.
- A pack handing over to the next — Christmas to New Year — does not overwrite that
  record, so January puts back what December borrowed rather than the Christmas
  charms.
- Choosing a charm yourself ends the season's claim, and the season is remembered as
  overruled so the two do not take turns every hour for the rest of October. That is
  forgotten when the season ends, so next year is offered afresh.

The calendar is consulted **once an hour** from the overlay's own tick. A season
begins at midnight and lasts weeks; an hour of lateness on a charm is not a defect,
and a check that ran more often would be looking for something that cannot have
happened.

## In the Library

Every seasonal charm has a Library entry with a region, a description, tags and a
preview, in a **Seasonal** category — so search, favourites and the rest work on
them exactly as on the collection, all year round, whether or not the season is on.
