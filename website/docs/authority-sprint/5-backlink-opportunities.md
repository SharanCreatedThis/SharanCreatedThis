# 5 — Backlink opportunities

_Places Hangly genuinely belongs, ranked by expected authority value._

**No outreach templates here, and no scaled link building.** Everything below is either a place
where submitting your own work is the intended use, or a community where the only way in is to
have something worth posting. Where a listing exists purely to sell links, it is excluded.

The binding constraint from `docs/final-scoring.md` stands: **Hangly currently has no inbound
links from anywhere.** That is the single largest limit on the site, and it is the only major
item that cannot be fixed by writing code.

---

## Tier 1 — do these first

### 1. Product Hunt
Free desktop app, genuine design work, a real story about the physics and the ARM64 build.
Product Hunt links are nofollow, so the SEO value is indirect — but the *referral* value and the
secondary coverage a good launch attracts is where the real authority comes from.
**Do it once and properly**, with the charm artwork as the visual. A second launch for a minor
version wastes the one shot.
**Value: high. Effort: medium. One-time.**

### 2. Hacker News — Show HN
Only worth doing with the engineering story, not the app pitch. Two candidates:
the Windows-on-ARM detection trap, and the connected-artwork problem (the thread ends at each
charm's own loop, so it cannot be derived).
HN links are nofollow but HN readers write blogs that are not.
**Value: high, variance also high. Effort: low. One-time.**

### 3. Awesome-mac and similar curated GitHub lists
`awesome-mac`, `awesome-macos-apps` and their relatives accept pull requests for apps that fit.
Hangly fits cleanly under desktop customisation. These are dofollow, permanently indexed, and
copied widely — a listing propagates into dozens of mirrors.
**Value: high. Effort: low. Do this first — it is an afternoon.**

### 4. Alternative-to directories
AlternativeTo, and the "alternatives" sections of the competitors themselves. OpenPets already
publishes an alternatives hub that is more honest about rivals than most commercial sites manage;
being listed there is legitimate and mutual.
**Value: medium-high. Effort: low.**

---

## Tier 2 — communities where the work has to earn it

### 5. r/macapps
Active, and specifically receptive to free indie Mac apps. Read the rules first; self-promotion
is tolerated when it is clearly disclosed and the app is genuinely free.
**Value: medium-high referral, low direct SEO. Effort: low.**

### 6. r/MacOS, r/Windows11, r/SideProject
Secondary. r/SideProject in particular rewards the build story more than the product.

### 7. Indian tech and design communities
The Tamil Divine and protection collections are a genuine differentiator here, and nothing else
in this category serves them. Indian design newsletters, Tamil developer communities and regional
tech press are a real fit rather than a stretch.
**Value: medium, and uncontested. Effort: medium.**

### 8. Designer and illustration communities
Dribbble, Behance — you already have a Behance profile — and design newsletters. The charm
artwork stands on its own without the app. The visual archive asset in
`4-citation-assets.md` is built for exactly this.
**Value: medium-high, and these audiences link. Effort: low, the assets exist.**

---

## Tier 3 — steady, lower ceiling

### 9. Mac software directories
MacUpdate, Softpedia, MacDownload, and the long tail. Mostly low-authority and some are
low-quality, but they are the established route for desktop software and they are how several
competitors got their first links. Submit to the reputable ones; ignore anything asking for
payment for a listing.
**Value: low-medium each, cumulative. Effort: low but repetitive.**

### 10. Newsletters
Indie Hackers, macOS-focused newsletters, and design weeklies. Most take submissions. A free app
with original artwork is an easy inclusion for a curator who needs items.
**Value: medium referral. Effort: low.**

### 11. Open-source adjacency
Not a link source directly, but: several charm competitors are open-source projects on GitHub
(`HangOn`, `EvilEye-dangling-charm`). Publishing something genuinely reusable — the charm SVG
set under a clear licence, or the build-time manifest resolver — earns links from repositories
rather than from directories, and those are durable.
**Value: medium-high, slow. Effort: medium. Requires a licensing decision.**

### 12. Cultural and interest communities
The nazar, daruma, maneki-neko and drishti bommai have audiences that have nothing to do with
software. A genuinely good page on what a drishti bommai is can be linked from cultural sites,
which is the most valuable and least contested link type available here — but **only if the page
is written for that audience rather than as a download funnel**.
**Value: high, slow, entirely dependent on content quality. Effort: high.**

## What not to do

- **No paid links, no link exchanges, no guest-post networks.** The site's entire position is
  that its claims are verifiable. Buying links contradicts that and is detectable.
- **No mass directory submission.** Ten good listings beat two hundred.
- **No outreach asking for a link.** Ask for a look, or ask nothing. The assets in
  `4-citation-assets.md` exist so the link is the natural response rather than the request.
- **Do not launch on Product Hunt until `/charms` exists.** The launch sends traffic to a product
  page that lists 55 of 75 charms. Fix the shop window first.
