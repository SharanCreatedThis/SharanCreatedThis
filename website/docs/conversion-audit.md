## Findings

Ordered by what they cost, not by how easy they are to fix.

---

### 1. CRITICAL — Vision's primary download button does not download

**Severity: critical. Needs a decision before it can be fixed.**

The "Download Vision" button on `/products/vision` renders as `href="#release"`. A click
handler intercepts it, opens a dialog, and fires a `download_intent` event with
`reason: "not_yet_released"`. The source comment reads:

> *Not a download: there is no build to download yet, and the dialog says so.*

That comment is now contradicted by the rest of the system:

| Evidence | State |
| --- | --- |
| `public/products/vision/Vision-1.1.dmg` | present, 17.8 MB |
| `/products/vision/download` | 302 → the DMG, live |
| `public/products/vision/appcast.xml` | serves 1.1 with an EdDSA signature |
| `SoftwareApplication` schema for Vision | `downloadUrl` set, `offers.availability: InStock`, `price: 0` |
| `/download`, `/products`, `/contact` | all link to the Vision download as available |
| `/products/vision` itself | says it is not released |

There is no reading of this where everything is correct. Either Vision 1.1 shipped and the
product page was never updated, or it has not shipped and five other pages plus the structured
data are advertising a product that is not available — which is a rich-results problem as well
as a conversion one, because `availability: InStock` on an unavailable product is exactly the
kind of claim that earns a manual action.

There is also a secondary effect: `#release` does not exist as an element anywhere in the
exported page, so with JavaScript disabled or before hydration the primary CTA is a dead anchor.

**Recommended fix — but pick the branch first:**

- *If Vision 1.1 is released:* point `DOWNLOAD` at `/products/vision/download`, delete the
  intent-tracking handler, and keep `trackDownload` in its place. One-line change.
- *If it is not released:* remove Vision's `downloadUrl` and `offers` from the schema, change
  `availability`, and soften the Vision CTAs on `/download`, `/products` and `/contact` to link
  to the product page rather than the download route.

---

### 2. HIGH — `/products/vision` is a conversion dead end

1,563 words and two internal links, one of which is the site root. It does not reach the
download route, the changelog, the install page or Hangly. A reader who lands here from search
— and it is the second most substantial product page on the site — has almost nowhere to go.

**Recommended fix:** give the Vision page the shared `SectionFooter`, or a tailored equivalent.
It is the same fix that took `/compare`, `/guides` and `/faq` from a handful of inbound links
to a median of 24.

---

### 3. HIGH — `/products/hangly/privacy` has one outbound link and no CTA

742 words, one internal link, back to the product page. Privacy pages get read by exactly the
sort of cautious buyer who is close to converting, and this one drops them.

**Recommended fix:** add the shared footer, and a single line at the end linking to the download
and the install page.

---

### 4. MEDIUM — the first download CTA on the home page sits at 66% depth

`/` carries a download link, but the first one appears two thirds of the way down the markup.
The home page is an editorial portfolio page first, which is a legitimate choice, but a visitor
arriving on a product-intent query has to scroll a long way before the site offers them anything.

**Recommended fix:** one product-aware CTA in the upper third — not a banner, a line. The
existing design language supports it.

---

### 5. MEDIUM — `/products/hangly`'s first CTA at 62% is misleading as a measurement

The Hangly page carries a platform-detecting `DownloadButton` in its hero, which renders as a
`<button>` rather than an `<a>` until client-side detection resolves. The measurement above
counts hrefs, so it undercounts this page.

This is worth recording rather than fixing: the button is correct behaviour, and the note here
exists so the next person reading this table does not "fix" a page that is already right. It
does mean, though, that the hero CTA is invisible to any crawler or answer engine that does not
execute JavaScript — worth knowing when judging whether an engine can see that Hangly is free.

---

### 6. MEDIUM — `/products/vision/docs` has one internal link

584 words, one outbound link. Same problem as the privacy page, same fix.

---

### 7. LOW — no CTA anywhere on the site names a price

Every download button says "Download". None says "free". "Free" is the single strongest word
available here and it is doing no work in any button label. The supporting text under the
buttons does say it, which is why this is low rather than medium.

**Recommended fix:** test "Download free for Mac" against the current label. Cheap to try.

---

### 8. LOW — mobile usability

No horizontal-scroll or tap-target problems were found by inspection: the layout uses a fluid
grid throughout, tables are wrapped in `.comparison-table-wrap` / `.hub-table-wrap` with
`overflow-x: auto`, and the changelog anchor links are hidden below 640px rather than becoming
unreachable tap targets.

This is an inspection result, not a measurement. A real verification needs Lighthouse on a
throttled mobile profile against the live site, which is the recommended next step rather than
a finding.
